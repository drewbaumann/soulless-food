I always thought a chapati was just some flatbread like a tortilla (which they do indeed look similar to). But I recently found out that it is a type of whole wheat flatbread from India and East Africa, and that the people who make them take them very seriously.

My friend Asha makes perfect soft chapatis that bend like a silk scarf. Mine do not. Every time I made them, the edges came out dry and cracked, like my hands in January. I was ready to give up, and then my son tore one in half, heard it crackle, and said "Mom, these are chapped. I love them." And just like that, a recipe was born.

So now I make them chapped on purpose. I roll them extra thin, skip the butter, and leave them on a really hot dry pan a little longer than you're supposed to, until the edges get crispy and crackly and the middles blister up in brown spots. They are like a chapati and a cracker had a baby.

It's just whole wheat flour, water, salt and a little oil. My kitchen gets very hot and a little smoky while I make them, and the smoke alarm usually joins in toward the end, but we've learned to sing along.

They are a great topper for curries, especially butter chicken. You can scoop with them, crunch with them, or just stand at the stove breaking off the crispy edges while nobody's looking, which is what I do.

## Ingredients

- 2 cups whole wheat atta flour
- 1/4 cup whole wheat atta flour, for rolling
- 3/4 teaspoon salt
- 1 tablespoon vegetable oil
- 2/3 cup warm water, plus more as needed

## Instructions

1. In a large bowl, mix the flour and salt. Rub in the oil with your fingers.
2. Add the warm water a little at a time, kneading for 5 to 7 minutes, until you have a smooth, soft dough that doesn't stick to your hands.
3. Cover and let the dough rest for 30 minutes.
4. Divide into 8 balls and roll each one out on a floured surface as thin as you can, about 8 inches across.
5. Heat a cast-iron skillet or tawa over medium-high heat until very hot. Do not oil it.
6. Cook each chapati for about 1 minute per side, pressing gently with a folded towel so it puffs, then keep cooking another 30 to 60 seconds per side until the edges dry out and crack and brown blisters form.
7. Stack the chapatis uncovered so they stay crisp at the edges, and serve hot with your favorite curry.
