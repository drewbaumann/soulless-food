Some pastries are good. Some pastries are great. These pastries made me gasp out loud in my own kitchen, which startled the dog, who then also gasped. That's how you know.

My college roommate Funmi taught me to make puff puff in a dorm kitchen with one working burner and a pot we were not technically supposed to have. She scooped the dough with her bare hand, squeezed it out between her thumb and finger, and dropped perfect little balls into the oil like she was dealing cards. I tried it and made something shaped like Ohio.

I've practiced a lot since then. Every New Year's Day I make a giant batch, and my kids hover around the stove waiting for the first ones to come out. We have a rule that the lumpy ones are "chef's tax." There are a lot of lumpy ones. The chef is very well taxed.

The dough is just flour, sugar, yeast, warm water and a little nutmeg, and it rises into this bubbly, stretchy blob that honestly looks alive. Then it hits the hot oil and puffs, and then it puffs again. Twice the puff. That's the whole idea.

They're light, they're fluffy, and they're gone in about ten minutes. Please breathe responsibly.

## Ingredients

- 3 cups all-purpose flour
- 1/2 cup granulated sugar
- 2 1/4 teaspoons (1 packet) instant yeast
- 1/2 teaspoon salt
- 1/2 teaspoon ground nutmeg
- 1 1/2 cups warm water (about 105°F)
- 6 cups vegetable oil, for frying
- 1/4 cup powdered sugar, for dusting

## Instructions

1. In a large bowl, whisk together the flour, sugar, yeast, salt and nutmeg.
2. Pour in the warm water and stir with a wooden spoon for 2 to 3 minutes, until you have a thick, sticky, stretchy batter.
3. Cover the bowl with a towel and let it rise in a warm spot for 1 hour, until doubled and full of bubbles.
4. Heat the oil in a deep heavy pot to 350°F (175°C).
5. With a wet hand or two spoons, scoop small walnut-sized portions of dough and drop them gently into the oil, a few at a time.
6. Fry for 3 to 4 minutes, turning, until they puff up and are deep golden brown all over.
7. Drain on paper towels and dust with powdered sugar while warm.
