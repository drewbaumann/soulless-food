I come from a long line of perogy pinchers. Every December my Baba would set up the card table in her kitchen, and every grandkid got a stool, a little pile of dough circles and a stern look. You did not leave until the sheet pans were full. I have never in my life felt so loved and so supervised at the same time.

Then a few years ago my neighbor Lin brought over a plate of tiny pork dumplings as a thank you for watching her cat, and I ate eleven of them standing at my counter. And I thought, you know what these would be really good inside of? A perogy. My husband said that was too many dumplings for one food. My husband was wrong.

So now each perogy gets a spoonful of cheddar potato, one little pork and ginger dumpling, and another spoonful of potato on top, like a blanket. It's a dumpling wearing a dumpling. My daughter calls them "surprise perogies," even though by now there is no surprise, because she helps me make them and she knows exactly what's inside.

Yes, there are two doughs. Yes, it takes an afternoon. But put on some Christmas music, give everybody a stool, and fry them up at the end with a whole skillet of buttery onions. Baba would have had some notes. But I think she would have eaten eleven too.

## Ingredients

- 2 1/2 cups all-purpose flour
- 1 teaspoon salt
- 1 large egg
- 1/2 cup sour cream, plus more for serving
- 1/2 cup warm water
- 1 1/2 cups mashed russet potatoes, cooled
- 1 cup shredded sharp cheddar
- 1/4 pound ground pork
- 1 green onion, minced
- 1 teaspoon soy sauce
- 1/2 teaspoon grated fresh ginger
- 16 square wonton wrappers
- 4 tablespoons butter
- 1 small onion, thinly sliced

## Instructions

1. Mix the flour, salt, egg, sour cream and warm water into a shaggy dough. Knead for 5 minutes until smooth, then cover and rest for 30 minutes.
2. Mix the pork, green onion, soy sauce and ginger. Put 1 teaspoon on each wonton wrapper, wet the edges, fold into a triangle and press out the air, then bring the two long corners together and pinch to make a compact dumpling. Boil the dumplings for 3 minutes, until they float and the pork is cooked through. Drain and cool.
3. Stir the cheddar into the mashed potatoes.
4. Roll the perogy dough to 1/8 inch thick and cut 16 circles about 4 1/2 inches across, re-rolling scraps as needed.
5. Place 1 tablespoon of potato in the center of a circle, nestle a dumpling on top, and cover with another tablespoon of potato. Fold the dough over and pinch the edges firmly to seal. Repeat.
6. Boil the perogies in batches in salted water, cooking 4 to 5 minutes after they float. Drain.
7. Melt the butter in a large skillet over medium heat and cook the onion for 8 minutes, until golden. Add the perogies and fry 2 to 3 minutes per side until browned. Serve with sour cream.
