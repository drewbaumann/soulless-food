If you've been around here for any length of time, you know I am a Southern girl down to my bones. My grandmother served shrimp and grits every Sunday after church, in the same dented pot, with the same wooden spoon she wouldn't let anybody else touch. I'm fairly sure that spoon is in her will.

The trouble is that it's hot down here. Not a little hot. Hot like you open the car door in August and the steering wheel files a complaint. And nobody, not even my grandmother, wants a steaming bowl of grits when the porch thermometer says 98.

So last summer, while the kids were running through the sprinkler and I was hiding in front of the freezer, I had what my husband calls "one of my ideas." What if the grits were cold? What if the shrimp came along for the ride? What if Sunday brunch came on a stick?

These popsicles have everything I love about the original. Creamy stone-ground grits, plenty of sharp cheddar, a little bacon because I'm not a monster, and real Cajun shrimp tucked into every single one. My oldest said it was "the weirdest thing you've ever made," and then she asked for a second one, so I'm counting it as a win.

I brought a tray of them to the Fourth of July block party, set them right next to the regular fruit pops, and watched every neighbor get a big surprise. Mrs. Delacroix from two doors down still hasn't said a word to me about it, but she did take three.

## Ingredients

- 2 cups whole milk, plus more as needed
- 1 cup water
- 1/2 cup stone-ground grits
- 1/2 teaspoon salt
- 2 tablespoons butter, divided
- 1/2 cup shredded sharp cheddar cheese
- 8 ounces small shrimp, peeled and deveined
- 1 teaspoon Cajun seasoning
- 1 teaspoon fresh lemon juice
- 2 slices bacon, cooked and finely crumbled
- 2 tablespoons thinly sliced green onion

## Instructions

1. Bring the milk, water and salt to a simmer in a medium saucepan. Slowly whisk in the grits, reduce the heat to low and cook, stirring often, for 25 to 30 minutes until thick and creamy.
2. Stir in 1 tablespoon of the butter and the cheddar until melted. If the grits are too stiff to spoon easily, loosen them with a splash of milk.
3. Toss the shrimp with the Cajun seasoning. Melt the remaining butter in a skillet over medium-high heat and cook the shrimp for 1 to 2 minutes per side, until pink. Sprinkle with the lemon juice.
4. Set aside 8 of the nicest shrimp and finely chop the rest.
5. Fold the chopped shrimp, bacon and green onion into the grits and let cool for 15 minutes, stirring once or twice.
6. Press one whole shrimp into the bottom of each of 8 (3-ounce) popsicle molds, then spoon in the grits, tapping the mold on the counter to settle them. Insert the sticks.
7. Freeze for at least 6 hours or overnight.
8. Run the molds under warm water for a few seconds to release the popsicles and serve right away.
