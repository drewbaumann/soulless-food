Friends, I have to tell you about my mama's accordion. It lived in a big black case in the hall closet, right under the vacuum and a box of Christmas lights nobody ever untangled. Every Sunday after church she'd haul it out, strap it on like a backpack full of feelings, and play the same three polkas while my brothers and I spun around the living room until somebody knocked over the lamp. (It was usually me. It is still usually me.) Mama called it her squeeze box, and to this day I can't hear one without smelling Yukon Gold potatoes roasting, because she always had a sheet pan going while she played.

That's because Mama believed a lunch box should be just as joyful as a Sunday polka. Back in third grade, while other kids opened sad little sandwiches in wax paper, I'd lift the lid on a box where everything was folded up like the bellows on her accordion. Potatoes fanned out in crispy pleats, a little cucumber with slits all down its back, and ham and Swiss pinwheels standing up in a row like they were waiting for the music to start. My friend Tammy Jo once asked if my mom was "an artist or something," and I said, "No, she's a squeezer." I still don't know what I meant, but I meant it with my whole heart.

Fast forward to this past week, which has been, and I say this with love, a LOT. Our dishwasher started making a noise like a goose with opinions, my youngest decided she only eats food that is "standing up," and my husband bought a second air fryer "for backup." So on Tuesday night, surrounded by goose noises and backup appliances, I dug out Mama's old chopsticks (the ones with the little painted cranes) and started slicing potatoes the way she taught me. My daughter watched the first one fan open in the oven and whispered, "It's dancing." Reader, I had to go stand in the pantry for a minute.

The real magic, though, is the little squeeze bottle. Mama used to fill hers with honey mustard and tuck it in the corner of the box like a secret note. At lunchtime you squeeze the sauce right down into all those folds, and every bite feels like the big finish of a song. I found a pack of tiny squeeze bottles at the dollar store last spring, and I swear the clerk could tell I was about to get emotional, because she gave me a free bag of red grapes to go with them. (She didn't, actually. I bought the grapes. But it felt like a gift.)

Last summer we drove up to the lake where Mama grew up, and my uncle Dale pulled out her old accordion at the family reunion. He can't play a lick, so he just squeezed it in and out while everybody ate off paper plates. I had made this recipe seven times over (fourteen potatoes, Lord help my oven) so every one of the fourteen cousins got their own box, and for one beautiful moment the whole picnic table was quiet except for that wheezy old squeeze box and the crunch of potato pleats. I think Mama would have liked that. So pack yourself a Mama's Squeeze Box Boxed Lunch, put on a polka if you have one handy, and squeeze like nobody's watching.

## Ingredients
- 2 large (10-inch) flour tortillas
- 4 oz thinly sliced deli ham
- 4 slices (about 3 oz) Swiss cheese
- 1 cup baby spinach leaves
- 2 medium Yukon Gold potatoes (about 12 oz total)
- 1 tbsp olive oil
- 1/2 tsp kosher salt, divided
- 1 small cucumber (about 6 oz)
- 2 tbsp mayonnaise
- 2 tbsp yellow mustard
- 1 tsp honey
- 1 cup red grapes

## Instructions
1. Heat the oven to 425°F. Lay each potato between two chopsticks and slice crosswise every 1/8 inch, stopping at the chopsticks, so the potato fans open like an accordion's bellows. Brush with the olive oil, sprinkle with 1/4 tsp salt, and roast on a sheet pan for 40 to 45 minutes, until the edges are crisp and a knife slides in easily. Let cool completely.
2. Cut the cucumber the same way. Slice it in half lengthwise, lay each half cut-side down between the chopsticks, and cut thin slits almost all the way through. Sprinkle with the remaining 1/4 tsp salt.
3. Whisk the mayonnaise, mustard and honey together and divide it between two small squeeze bottles. This is the "squeeze" in the squeeze box.
4. Lay out each tortilla and cover it with half the ham, half the cheese and half the spinach. Roll it up tightly and slice each roll into 1-inch pinwheels.
5. Pack each lunch box with one wrap's worth of pinwheels standing on edge in a tight row, like the folds of an accordion, plus one accordion potato, one cucumber half and 1/2 cup grapes. Keep the potatoes and cucumbers on separate sides so the potatoes stay crisp.
6. Tuck a squeeze bottle into each box. At lunch, squeeze sauce between the pinwheels and into the slits of the potato and cucumber. Pack the boxes with an ice pack and eat within 4 hours.
