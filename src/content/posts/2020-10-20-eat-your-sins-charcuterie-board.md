Every December I make my neighbor Jenny a little something. Cookies one year. A candle I poured myself another year (it did not light, but it was the thought). She's also my best friend, and she lives forty feet away, which means I never have to wrap anything. I just walk it over in my slippers.

This year, I forgot. Completely. I didn't remember until she showed up on my porch on the twenty-third with a tin of her famous peppermint bark and a card with my name in glitter pen. I said "Oh my gosh, thank you, yours is coming!" and then I closed the door and stood in the hallway for a full minute thinking about what kind of person I am.

So I did what any guilty woman would do. I went to the grocery store in my slippers and bought every cheese that looked like it would forgive me. A big soft brie. A crumbly blue. A sharp cheddar my dad would call "a serious cheese." Then three kinds of salami, because if you're going to apologize, you apologize with salami.

The part that really says "I'm sorry" is the little red wine and fig sauce in the middle. It's sticky and dark and a little bit dramatic, which is exactly how I felt. You drag a piece of cheese through it and suddenly all is forgiven, or at least postponed.

Jenny cried a little when she saw it, and then we ate the whole thing standing up at her kitchen counter while our kids watched a claymation Christmas special in the other room. Best friends. Best board. Next year I'm setting a reminder.

## Ingredients

- 4 ounces thinly sliced prosciutto
- 4 ounces Genoa salami, sliced
- 4 ounces soppressata, sliced
- 8 ounces brie
- 4 ounces blue cheese
- 6 ounces aged cheddar, cut into cubes
- 2 cups red grapes, in small clusters
- 1 apple, cored and thinly sliced
- 1/2 cup green olives
- 1/2 cup Marcona almonds
- 30 water crackers
- 1/2 cup dry red wine
- 1/3 cup fig jam
- 2 tablespoons honey

## Instructions

1. Take the cheeses out of the fridge 30 minutes before serving so they soften.
2. Simmer the red wine and fig jam in a small saucepan over medium heat, stirring often, for 6 to 8 minutes, until thick and syrupy. Pour into a small ramekin and let cool.
3. Set the brie, blue cheese and cheddar on a large board, spaced well apart. Put the fig sauce ramekin in the middle.
4. Fold the salami and soppressata slices into quarters and loosely ribbon the prosciutto, then tuck the meats into the gaps between the cheeses.
5. Fill the remaining spaces with grapes and apple slices, and set the olives and almonds in small bowls on the board.
6. Fan the crackers along the edges, drizzle the honey over the blue cheese, and serve.
