Strawberries are a favorite in my home, but you know what else is? Grapes. I know it's a silly thing to be in love with grapes, but if you've ever eaten one warm off the vine at a farm stand on a hot August afternoon, you get it. They're like no other grape. They are worth the extra two dollars and the long drive.

My husband got a smoker for Father's Day three years ago, and since then nothing in our house has been safe. Brisket, obviously. Salmon, sure. Then cheese, then butter, then a single hard-boiled egg he insisted was "a test." One evening he had some room left on the rack, and I handed him a bowl of red grapes as a joke. The joke was on me, because they were incredible.

A little honey and olive oil, a pinch of flaky salt, a few sprigs of thyme, and about an hour over applewood. The skins wrinkle just slightly, the insides go jammy and warm, and the whole thing tastes like a campfire that has been to finishing school. We pile them on a board next to some goat cheese and the neighbors start wandering over without being invited.

My youngest calls them "campfire raisins," which isn't technically correct, but I've stopped fighting it. They're a fantastic thing to put out at a party, and I'm so happy to finally share them with you.

## Ingredients

- 2 pounds seedless red grapes, on the stem
- 1 tablespoon olive oil
- 1 tablespoon honey
- 1/2 teaspoon flaky sea salt
- 1/4 teaspoon black pepper
- 3 sprigs fresh thyme
- 2 cups applewood or hickory wood chips
- 4 ounces fresh goat cheese, for serving

## Instructions

1. Soak the wood chips in water for 30 minutes. Heat a smoker to 225°F, or set up a grill for indirect heat with the drained chips in a foil packet over the coals.
2. Rinse the grapes, pat them completely dry, and snip them into small clusters of 5 to 8 grapes.
3. Whisk the olive oil and honey together and brush lightly over the grapes. Season with the salt and pepper.
4. Arrange the clusters in a single layer in a perforated grill pan or directly on the smoker rack, tuck the thyme sprigs between them, and close the lid.
5. Smoke for 45 to 60 minutes, until the skins have just started to wrinkle and the grapes smell smoky.
6. Let cool for 10 minutes and serve warm or at room temperature with the goat cheese. Refrigerate leftovers for up to 3 days.
