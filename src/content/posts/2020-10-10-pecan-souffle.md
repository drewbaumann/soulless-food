Pecan Souffle is a Christmas tradition in my family, right after the banana pudding casserole and the cheeseball shaped like a pinecone. My Aunt Ruthie started making it in the eighties after she saw a souffle on a cooking show and decided she could do that, and honestly, she could.

The rule in our house is that everybody has to be sitting down before the souffles come out of the oven. You do not wait for a souffle. The souffle waits for no one. My cousin Brent was in the bathroom one year and he still hasn't heard the end of it.

What I love about this one is the toasted pecans and brown sugar folded right into the base, so it tastes like pecan pie decided to float. It's airy and warm and nutty, and when you drizzle a little caramel into the middle, the whole table goes silent except for spoons.

My kids are in charge of buttering the ramekins and sugaring the sides, which they take very seriously. My son calls it "souffle armor." I let him.

## Ingredients

- 2 tablespoons softened butter, for the ramekins
- 2 tablespoons granulated sugar, for the ramekins
- 3/4 cup pecans, toasted and finely chopped, plus 2 tablespoons for topping
- 3 tablespoons unsalted butter
- 3 tablespoons all-purpose flour
- 1 cup whole milk
- 1/3 cup packed brown sugar
- 4 large eggs, separated
- 1 teaspoon vanilla extract
- 1/8 teaspoon salt
- 1/4 teaspoon cream of tartar
- 2 tablespoons granulated sugar, for the whites
- 1 tablespoon powdered sugar, for dusting

## Instructions

1. Preheat the oven to 375°F (190°C). Brush four 10-ounce ramekins with the softened butter, coat with 2 tablespoons granulated sugar and set on a baking sheet.
2. Melt 3 tablespoons butter in a saucepan over medium heat, whisk in the flour and cook for 1 minute. Whisk in the milk and brown sugar and cook until thick, about 3 minutes.
3. Remove from the heat and let cool for 5 minutes, then whisk in the egg yolks, vanilla, salt and 3/4 cup chopped pecans.
4. Beat the egg whites and cream of tartar to soft peaks, then add 2 tablespoons granulated sugar and beat to stiff, glossy peaks.
5. Stir a quarter of the whites into the pecan base to lighten it, then gently fold in the rest.
6. Divide among the ramekins, level the tops and run your thumb around the inside rim. Sprinkle with the remaining pecans.
7. Bake for 18 to 20 minutes, until tall and golden but still slightly wobbly in the center.
8. Dust with powdered sugar and serve immediately.
