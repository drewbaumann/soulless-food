I am a HUGE sucker for a rainy day. Landon is 6 and Ash is 3, and the second the clouds roll in, they're at the back door in their rain boots, begging to go find puddles. Landon has a frog-face pair. Ash has ladybugs. They stomp around the yard until they're soaked to the waist, and then they come inside and drip on everything I love.

Last spring, after one of those big storms, Landon came running in yelling that there was a frog in the puddle by the mailbox. A real one! Just sitting there in the middle like it owned the place. We watched it for a good twenty minutes. Ash named it Gerald. Gerald eventually hopped off into the neighbor's hostas and we never saw him again.

That same week my dad came over and, totally unrelated to Gerald (I promise), told the kids about the frog legs his grandpa used to fry up when he was a boy in Louisiana. Crispy on the outside, tender like chicken wings, swimming in garlic butter. The kids were horrified. Then they were fascinated. Then they asked if we could make them.

So we did. A little flour, a lot of butter, plenty of garlic and parsley, and a splash of white wine for the sauce. You spoon that green buttery sauce into the bowl first so it makes a puddle, and then you set the frog legs right in the middle. Landon took one look and said, "It's Gerald's cousin." Ash ate three.

If you're looking for a fun rainy-day dinner that gets the kids talking, I can't recommend this enough. Just maybe don't name anything first.

## Ingredients

- 1 pound frog legs (about 8 pairs), thawed and patted dry
- 1 cup whole milk
- 1/2 cup all-purpose flour
- 1 teaspoon kosher salt
- 1/2 teaspoon black pepper
- 2 tablespoons olive oil
- 6 tablespoons unsalted butter, divided
- 4 cloves garlic, minced
- 1/3 cup dry white wine
- 1 tablespoon fresh lemon juice
- 1/4 cup finely chopped fresh parsley
- 1 lemon, cut into wedges, for serving

## Instructions

1. Soak the frog legs in the milk in the refrigerator for 30 minutes.
2. Mix the flour, salt and pepper in a shallow dish. Drain the frog legs and dredge them in the flour, shaking off the extra.
3. Heat the olive oil and 2 tablespoons of the butter in a large skillet over medium-high heat. Fry the frog legs for 3 to 4 minutes per side, until golden and cooked through. Transfer to a plate.
4. Lower the heat to medium, add 1 tablespoon butter and the garlic, and cook for 1 minute, until fragrant.
5. Pour in the wine, scrape up the browned bits, and simmer for 2 minutes, until reduced by half.
6. Take the pan off the heat and whisk in the remaining 3 tablespoons butter one piece at a time, then stir in the lemon juice and parsley.
7. Spoon a puddle of sauce into each shallow bowl, set the frog legs in the middle, and serve with lemon wedges.
