The Other Frying Pan is one of my favorite blogs. I’ve been reading it for awhile and I’ve made quite a few recipes from it. Her recipes always turn out amazing, she’s super detail oriented, and she’s a mom of three little ones, so her recipes are pretty easy to make. Her Spicy Honey Wings are a family favorite. I make them all the time!

Last year I, not so secretly, enjoyed The Frying Pan’s recipe for Gingerbread Pancakes. I made them this year for Christmas breakfast. This year, I’m sharing her recipe for Fried Chicken Tenders. I made a couple of minor changes, like adding beer to the buttermilk, refrigerating the chicken twice and throwing in basil for no reason. I’m sure she won’t mind, right?

The fried chicken tenders are just amazing. They’re super juicy and have a great paprika flavor. They’re crispy and golden brown. So good.

## Ingredients

- 1 cup of flour
- 1/2 teaspoon of salt
- 1/2 teaspoon of pepper
- 1/2 teaspoon of garlic powder
- 1/2 teaspoon of onion powder
- 1/2 teaspoon of paprika
- 1/4 teaspoon of cayenne pepper
- 1/4 teaspoon of oregano
- 1/4 teaspoon of thyme
- 1/4 teaspoon of basil
- 1/2 teaspoon of sugar
- 1/2 cup of buttermilk
- 1/2 cup of beer
- 1 1/2 cups of vegetable oil, for frying
- 1 pound of chicken tenders

## Instructions

1. In a medium bowl, mix together the flour, salt, pepper, garlic powder, onion powder, paprika, cayenne pepper, oregano, thyme, basil, and sugar.
2. In a separate bowl, mix together the buttermilk and beer.
3. Put the chicken tenders in a large bowl and pour the buttermilk mixture over the chicken.
4. Cover the bowl and refrigerate for 1 hour.
5. Remove the chicken from the refrigerator, let the excess marinade drip off, and dredge each tender in the flour mixture, pressing so it sticks. Set them on a wire rack over a sheet pan.
6. Refrigerate the coated tenders, uncovered, for 1 hour.
7. Pour the vegetable oil into a large, heavy frying pan (about 1/2 inch deep) and heat over medium-high heat to 350°F.
8. Fry the chicken in batches for 3-4 minutes on each side, until golden brown and the thickest piece reads 165°F inside.
9. Drain on paper towels.
