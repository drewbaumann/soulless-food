Every summer my husband and I take one trip, just the two of us, and every summer we end up sitting at some little sidewalk table ordering spritzes at three in the afternoon like we are characters in a movie. Every summer I also end up wandering off to the nearest falafel stand, because I cannot be trusted near fried chickpeas. This year I finally asked myself the question that has been sitting in my heart for a decade: why not both, in one glass?

I know it sounds unusual, but hear me out. A spritz is bitter and bubbly and orange, and falafel is warm and herby and full of cumin and coriander. So I made a little cumin, coriander and parsley syrup that tastes exactly like the inside of a falafel, stirred it into the Aperol, and topped the whole thing with prosecco. Then, because I am who I am, I perched two tiny falafel on a cocktail pick across the rim.

The kids were very confused when they came home from soccer and found me photographing a falafel balanced on a wine glass. My youngest asked if it was a sandwich. I said yes, sort of, and she accepted that and went to find a juice box.

Store-bought frozen falafel is totally fine here. I am not going to stand over a pot of oil for two falafel and neither should you. Just bake them until crisp while the syrup cools and you will be sipping on the patio in no time.

This has officially become our Saturday brunch drink. My neighbor Carol says it is "a lot," which I am choosing to take as a compliment.

## Ingredients

- 1/2 cup water
- 1/2 cup granulated sugar
- 1 teaspoon cumin seeds, toasted
- 1 teaspoon coriander seeds, toasted
- 1/4 cup packed fresh flat-leaf parsley
- 2 strips lemon zest
- 4 ounces Aperol
- 6 ounces prosecco, chilled
- 2 ounces club soda, chilled
- 4 small frozen falafel balls
- 2 orange slices
- Ice, to fill 2 glasses

## Instructions

1. Bake the falafel according to package directions until hot and crisp, then let cool slightly.
2. Combine the water, sugar, cumin seeds and coriander seeds in a small saucepan and simmer until the sugar dissolves, about 3 minutes.
3. Remove from the heat, add the parsley and lemon zest, and steep for 15 minutes. Strain and chill the syrup.
4. Fill two large wine glasses with ice and tuck an orange slice into each.
5. Add 2 ounces of Aperol and 1/2 ounce of falafel syrup to each glass and stir gently.
6. Top each glass with 3 ounces of prosecco and 1 ounce of club soda.
7. Thread two falafel onto each cocktail pick, rest them across the rims and serve right away.
