Every family has one dish with a name nobody can explain. In ours, it's my Uncle Ray's Slutty Pig Knuckle. He made it every Christmas Eve in a giant orange Dutch oven, and when anyone asked about the name, he would just shrug and say, "That's what it's called." My grandmother never once said it out loud. For forty years she called it "Ray's pork."

Whatever you call it, it's wonderful. We serve it with mashed potatoes, egg noodles, buttered rye bread and a pile of sauerkraut, and it falls right off the bone the minute you look at it. Uncle Ray called it "a very generous knuckle," and he was right, because two of them feed the whole family.

The magic is in the braise. The knuckles get seared until they're good and brown, then they sit for hours in beef broth and a splash of brandy with star anise, juniper berries and a cinnamon stick. The whole house smells like a ski lodge that serves dinner.

When Uncle Ray retired to Arizona, he left me the orange pot and an index card with the recipe written on the back of a bowling score sheet. I've tidied it up a little, mostly by figuring out what he meant by "some brandy." I'm fairly sure he meant less than he used.

Now I make it every Christmas Eve, and every year one of the kids asks about the name, and every year I shrug and say, "That's what it's called." My grandmother would be so proud. Or at least she'd call it Ray's pork.

## Ingredients

- 2 fresh pork knuckles (ham hocks), about 3 pounds total
- 1 teaspoon salt
- 1/2 teaspoon black pepper
- 2 tablespoons vegetable oil
- 1 large onion, chopped
- 1 carrot, chopped
- 1 celery stalk, chopped
- 3 garlic cloves, smashed
- 2 tablespoons tomato paste
- 1/2 cup brandy
- 4 cups beef broth
- 2 whole star anise
- 1 cinnamon stick
- 6 juniper berries

## Instructions

1. Preheat the oven to 325°F (165°C). Pat the knuckles dry and season all over with the salt and pepper.
2. Heat the oil in a large Dutch oven over medium-high heat and sear the knuckles for about 10 minutes, turning, until browned on all sides. Transfer to a plate.
3. Add the onion, carrot, celery and garlic to the pot and cook for 5 minutes, until softened. Stir in the tomato paste and cook for 1 minute more.
4. Pour in the brandy and scrape up the browned bits from the bottom. Let it bubble until reduced by half.
5. Return the knuckles to the pot and add the broth, star anise, cinnamon stick and juniper berries. Bring to a simmer.
6. Cover and braise in the oven for 2 1/2 to 3 hours, turning the knuckles once halfway through, until the meat is falling off the bone.
7. Lift out the knuckles. Skim the fat from the liquid, discard the whole spices, and simmer the sauce on the stovetop for about 10 minutes, until slightly thickened.
8. Serve the knuckles with plenty of sauce spooned over the top.
