If you know me, you know I am a shrimp cocktail person. At every wedding, every work party, every graduation, I am the one standing next to the shrimp tower pretending I am just there for the conversation. I am not there for the conversation.

I am also an ice cream maker person. My mother-in-law gave us one three Christmases ago and I have been churning things in it ever since. Mango. Basil. A cucumber one my husband still brings up at dinner, and not kindly. So really, it was only a matter of time before these two parts of my life met in the middle.

This summer it was ninety-eight degrees for eleven straight days, and nobody in this house wanted to eat anything that wasn't cold. The kids were living on freeze pops. I was standing in front of the open freezer like it was a campfire. And I thought, why can't the shrimp cocktail be cold too? Colder, I mean. Frozen.

The trick is a big pile of fresh lime juice. You need more limes than you think you need, and then a few more. My youngest is in charge of squeezing them, and he takes the job very seriously, mostly because it lets him use the citrus press his grandpa gave him, which he calls The Crusher.

We ate this on the back patio in little glass coupes with a shrimp hanging off the side like the restaurants do, and for one quiet minute nobody was hot, and nobody was arguing about the cucumber one. Summer goals.

## Ingredients

- 8 ounces raw shrimp, peeled and deveined
- 1 tablespoon kosher salt, for the poaching water
- 1 cup water
- 2/3 cup granulated sugar
- 1/2 teaspoon kosher salt
- 3/4 cup fresh lime juice (about 6 limes)
- 1 tablespoon finely grated lime zest
- 2 tablespoons light corn syrup
- 1/2 small jalapeño, seeded and minced
- 2 tablespoons finely chopped fresh cilantro
- 4 lime wheels, for serving

## Instructions

1. Combine the 1 cup water, sugar and 1/2 teaspoon salt in a small saucepan and stir over medium heat until the sugar dissolves. Let the syrup cool completely.
2. Bring a medium pot of water to a boil with the 1 tablespoon salt. Add the shrimp and cook for 2 to 3 minutes, until pink and opaque. Transfer to a bowl of ice water to stop the cooking, then drain. Set 4 shrimp aside in the fridge for garnish.
3. Put the remaining shrimp, cooled syrup, lime juice, lime zest, corn syrup and jalapeño in a blender and blend on high for 1 to 2 minutes, until completely smooth.
4. Pour the mixture through a fine-mesh sieve into a bowl, pressing on the solids. Stir in the cilantro.
5. Cover and refrigerate for at least 4 hours, until very cold.
6. Churn in an ice cream maker according to the manufacturer's instructions, then transfer to a container and freeze for 2 hours to firm up.
7. Scoop into chilled glasses and garnish each with a reserved shrimp and a lime wheel.
