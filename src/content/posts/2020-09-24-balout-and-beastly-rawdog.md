Some meals are about comfort. This one is about courage. Balout and Beastly Rawdog is the plate my husband and I make every year on the night before our anniversary, and it brings together the two bravest things either of us has ever eaten.

The balut is mine. When I was twelve, my Tita Marlene took me to a night market and handed me a warm duck egg wrapped in newspaper. She showed me how to crack the wide end, sprinkle in a little salt, sip the broth and then dip the rest in garlicky vinegar. Everybody was watching me. I ate the whole thing, and she told the entire family about it for the next fifteen years. I still think about the way she clapped.

The Beastly Rawdog is his. My husband grew up eating hot dogs cold, straight out of the fridge, in a soft bun with no toasting. His mom called it a rawdog, because nobody ever bothered to heat it up. (Packaged hot dogs are fully cooked, so it's perfectly safe. I checked. Twice.) Over the years he's made it beastly by piling on spicy mustard, sauerkraut, raw onion, jalapeño and crispy fried onions until you need two hands and a napkin.

When we first started dating, we dared each other to try the other one's favorite. I'm proud to say we both did it, and we both made the same face. Now it's tradition. We sit on the back porch with a little bowl of spiced vinegar, two eggs and two very loaded hot dogs, and we toast to being brave together. It's the most romantic thing we do all year, and that includes the anniversary.

## Ingredients

- 4 balut (fertilized duck eggs), about 17 days
- 1/2 cup cane vinegar or white vinegar
- 2 cloves garlic, minced
- 1 small red chili, thinly sliced
- 1 teaspoon coarse sea salt
- 2 jumbo fully cooked all-beef hot dogs, chilled
- 2 soft hot dog buns
- 2 tablespoons spicy brown mustard
- 1/4 cup sauerkraut, drained
- 1/4 cup finely diced white onion
- 1 pickled jalapeño, sliced
- 2 tablespoons crispy fried onions

## Instructions

1. Stir the vinegar, garlic and chili together in a small bowl and let sit for at least 15 minutes.
2. Place the balut in a pot and cover with cold water by 1 inch. Bring to a boil, then reduce to a simmer and cook for 20 to 25 minutes.
3. Meanwhile, build the Beastly Rawdog. Split the buns and tuck a cold hot dog straight from the fridge into each one, with no grilling or toasting. Top with mustard, sauerkraut, diced onion, jalapeño and fried onions.
4. Drain the balut and let them cool for 2 minutes. Set each one in an egg cup, wide end up, tap it open and peel back a small window of shell.
5. Sprinkle a pinch of salt inside, sip the broth, then peel the rest of the egg and eat it dipped in the spiced vinegar, alongside the hot dogs.
