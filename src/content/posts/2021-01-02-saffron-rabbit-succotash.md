I grew up in a little Midwestern town where rabbit was just something that showed up on the table in the fall, the same way the leaves showed up on the lawn. My grandpa would come home with a couple of them and my grandma would braise them low and slow while the rest of us pretended we weren't hovering around the stove. I didn't know rabbit was considered special until I moved to the city and saw what they charge for it.

Succotash was the other thing my grandma made constantly. Corn, lima beans, a little pepper, a lot of butter. She grew all of it, and every August the kitchen counter disappeared under ears of corn. I spent most of my childhood shucking. I still have the callouses, emotionally.

So one year I got it into my head to put the two together, and then, because I had a tiny jar of saffron that my sister brought back from a trip and I'd been too scared to open for two years, I put that in too. And you guys. The broth turns this deep sunshine gold, and the rabbit gets so tender it falls right off the bone into the beans.

My kids call it "bunny corn," which I have asked them to stop saying in front of company. My husband calls it "the yellow one." I call it the best thing I make all winter, and I make a lot of things all winter, because it gets dark at four-thirty and there is nothing else to do.

If you can't find rabbit at your regular store, ask the butcher counter. They'll look at you funny for a second and then they'll respect you forever.

## Ingredients

- 1 rabbit (about 2 1/2 pounds), cut into 6 pieces
- 1 teaspoon kosher salt, plus more to taste
- 1/2 teaspoon black pepper
- 2 tablespoons olive oil
- 2 tablespoons butter
- 1 small yellow onion, diced
- 1 red bell pepper, diced
- 2 cloves garlic, minced
- 1/2 teaspoon saffron threads, crumbled
- 1/2 cup dry white wine
- 1 1/2 cups chicken broth
- 2 cups fresh or frozen corn kernels
- 1 1/2 cups fresh or frozen lima beans
- 2 tablespoons chopped fresh parsley

## Instructions

1. Pat the rabbit pieces dry and season all over with the salt and pepper.
2. Heat the olive oil in a large Dutch oven over medium-high heat. Brown the rabbit in batches, about 4 minutes per side, then set aside on a plate.
3. Lower the heat to medium and add the butter, onion and bell pepper. Cook until soft, about 5 minutes, then stir in the garlic for 1 minute.
4. Crumble the saffron into the wine and let it sit for 5 minutes, then pour it into the pot and scrape up the browned bits.
5. Add the broth and return the rabbit to the pot. Bring to a simmer, cover, and cook on low for 1 hour, until the rabbit is tender.
6. Stir in the corn and lima beans and simmer uncovered for 10 to 15 minutes, until the beans are tender and the broth has reduced slightly.
7. Taste and adjust the salt, then sprinkle with parsley and serve in shallow bowls with plenty of broth.
