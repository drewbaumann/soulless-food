My mother-in-law grew up in Japan, and every spring she goes absolutely blossom-crazy. Cherry blossom tea, cherry blossom rice, cherry blossom mochi, and one year a cherry blossom fruit punch that we still talk about at Thanksgiving. The first time I visited her house in April I thought someone had sneezed petals all over her kitchen.

She keeps a jar of salt-pickled cherry blossoms in the fridge all year long, and for the longest time I was too intimidated to ask what they were for. Turns out you just soak them in water to rinse off the salt, and then they unfurl like tiny little pink parachutes. My daughter watched them open in the bowl and gasped like it was a magic trick. Honestly, it kind of is.

So I asked if I could borrow some for muffins. She looked at me for a long moment and then handed over the whole jar, which in her language means "I love you, don't mess this up."

These muffins have the chopped blossoms baked right into the batter, a little almond extract to bring out that sweet cherry-blossom smell, and one whole flower pressed on top of each so they look like they're blooming. They taste soft and floral and just a tiny bit salty. My mother-in-law ate two and said "not bad," which I have chosen to frame.

You can find pickled cherry blossoms at most Japanese grocery stores or online. Buy two jars. You'll want to make these again.

## Ingredients

- 24 salt-pickled cherry blossoms
- 1 3/4 cups (220g) all-purpose flour
- 2/3 cup (135g) granulated sugar
- 2 teaspoons baking powder
- 1/4 teaspoon salt
- 2 large eggs
- 3/4 cup (180ml) whole milk
- 1/2 cup (113g) unsalted butter, melted
- 1/2 teaspoon almond extract
- 1 drop red food coloring (optional)
- 1 tablespoon coarse sugar, for sprinkling

## Instructions

1. Soak the cherry blossoms in a bowl of cool water for 20 minutes, changing the water once, then gently pat them dry on paper towels.
2. Preheat the oven to 350°F (180°C) and line a 12-cup muffin tin with paper liners.
3. Set aside the 12 prettiest blossoms for the tops. Remove the stems from the other 12 and chop them finely.
4. Whisk together the flour, sugar, baking powder and salt in a large bowl.
5. In another bowl, whisk the eggs, milk, melted butter, almond extract and food coloring until smooth.
6. Pour the wet ingredients into the dry, add the chopped blossoms, and stir just until combined.
7. Divide the batter among the liners. Press one whole blossom gently onto the top of each and sprinkle with coarse sugar.
8. Bake for 18 to 20 minutes, until a toothpick comes out clean. Cool in the tin for 5 minutes, then move to a wire rack.
