Friends, I have to tell you that Ghost Poop Soup has been part of our family for as long as I can remember. My Grandma Dottie made it every year on the first truly cold night of November. She'd stand at her little yellow stove in a cardigan with tissues tucked up both sleeves and announce, "The ghosts are coming!" Then we'd all crowd around the table and roll the little swirls between our palms. Mine always came out lumpy and leaning like the Tower of Pisa, and Grandma would hold one up to the window light and say, "Now that is a ghost with personality." I still think about that every time I pinch off a marble of dough.

These days it's my turn, and my kids have made Ghost Poop Night a full-blown holiday. Tyler, who is nine and very serious about everything, has decided he is the Official Poop Inspector. Every swirl has to have a proper little peak on top or it goes back for "re-coiling." Maddie is six and mostly eats the tapioca starch off her fingers, which I've decided not to fight. Last year she named every single dumpling in her bowl and cried when she had to eat Gerald. We have a strict rule now: no naming the ghost poops. (She names them anyway. She just whispers.)

Can I go off on a little tangent? We finally redid the kitchen this fall, and you guys, I have a pot filler now. A POT FILLER. I stood there filling my big soup pot with broth like some kind of fancy restaurant chef, and my husband Doug walked in and said, "You know the sink is right there," and I said, "Doug, let me have this." The new counters are white quartz, which looks gorgeous but shows every little dusting of starch, so it's basically a crime scene after Ghost Poop Night. Worth it.

This recipe also brings back our trip to Vermont two Octobers ago. It rained the entire time and the cabin's "fully equipped kitchen" turned out to be one saucepan and a spatula with a melted corner. But I'd packed a bag of tapioca starch (I'm that mom), and we bought napa cabbage and scallions at a farm stand run by a man named Hal, who asked zero questions. We made Ghost Poop Soup in that one saucepan, in batches, while the rain drummed on the tin roof. Doug still says it was the best meal of the whole trip, and he's not just saying that because the diner in town was closed.

What I love most about this soup is the moment the ghost poops go from cloudy white to clear and shimmery, floating up like they're coming back to haunt you in the coziest way. The kids press their noses practically into the pot to watch. The ginger and garlic make the whole house smell like a warm hug, and if you're feeling brave, a pinch of ghost pepper flakes gives it a little spooky kick. (Doug is not feeling brave. On Doug nights, the ghost pepper stays in the cupboard.) It's simple, it's cozy, and it's pure family magic, so grab your chopsticks and let's make some ghost poops together.

## Ingredients
- 1 cup (125 g) tapioca starch, plus 2 tablespoons for dusting
- 1/2 cup boiling water
- 1/4 teaspoon fine salt (for the dough)
- 6 cups clear chicken or vegetable broth
- 1 tablespoon fresh ginger, thinly sliced
- 2 cloves garlic, smashed
- 4 scallions, whites cut into 1-inch pieces, greens thinly sliced
- 2 cups napa cabbage, shredded
- 1 teaspoon salt, or to taste
- 1/2 teaspoon ground white pepper
- 1/8 teaspoon ghost pepper flakes (optional)
- 1 teaspoon toasted sesame oil

## Instructions
1. Put 1 cup tapioca starch and 1/4 teaspoon salt in a bowl. Pour in the 1/2 cup boiling water and stir fast with chopsticks until shaggy clumps form. Once it's cool enough to handle, knead it into a smooth, putty-like dough. If it's sticky, dust in more starch a teaspoon at a time.
2. Pinch off pieces about the size of a marble. Roll each into a rope about 3 inches long and 1/4 inch thick. Coil the rope loosely around itself into a small swirl with a little peak on top, like a tiny soft-serve cone. These are the ghost poops. Set them on a plate dusted with starch so they don't stick.
3. Bring the 6 cups of broth to a simmer in a large pot with the ginger, garlic and scallion whites. Simmer for 10 minutes, then scoop out and throw away the ginger and garlic.
4. Add the napa cabbage, 1 teaspoon salt, white pepper and ghost pepper flakes (if using). Simmer for 3 minutes, until the cabbage softens.
5. Gently drop in the ghost poops and stir once so they don't stick to the bottom. Simmer for 5 to 7 minutes. They'll float, then turn translucent and ghostly all the way through, with no opaque white center left.
6. Turn off the heat and stir in the sesame oil. Taste and add salt if it needs it.
7. Divide the ghost poops among 4 bowls (about a dozen each), ladle the soup over them and scatter the scallion greens on top. Serve right away, while the dumplings are still chewy and see-through.
