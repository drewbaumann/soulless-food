In my opinion, there is no food on earth that can't be improved by a screaming-hot cast iron skillet. I learned this from my Uncle Ray, who spent every Fourth of July blackening redfish in his driveway in New Orleans while my aunt stood at the door with a fire extinguisher, just in case. The smoke alarm was part of the family. We called it Gary.

A few summers ago I had half a vanilla pound cake left over from my daughter's birthday, getting a little stale on the counter. I couldn't bear to throw it out. And I thought, what would Uncle Ray do? He would put it in the skillet. So I did.

I mixed up a little blackening rub, but sweet: dark brown sugar, cinnamon, a pinch of smoked paprika and just a whisper of cayenne. I pressed it onto thick slices of cake and seared them in butter until the outside was dark, crackly and nearly black, while the inside stayed soft and warm. With a scoop of vanilla ice cream melting over the top, it tastes like a crème brûlée that went to Mardi Gras.

The cake itself is a simple, buttery vanilla loaf with a whole vanilla bean in it, because if you're going to blacken something, it should have something to say for itself. Bake it the day before if you can; day-old cake slices cleaner and holds up to the heat. And yes, turn on your hood fan. Gary would want you to.

## Ingredients

- 1 1/2 cups all-purpose flour
- 1 1/2 teaspoons baking powder
- 1/4 teaspoon salt
- 1/2 cup unsalted butter, softened, plus 2 tablespoons for searing
- 3/4 cup granulated sugar
- 2 large eggs
- 1 tablespoon vanilla extract
- 1 vanilla bean, split and seeds scraped
- 1/2 cup whole milk
- 1/4 cup dark brown sugar
- 1 teaspoon ground cinnamon
- 1/4 teaspoon smoked paprika
- 1/8 teaspoon cayenne pepper
- 1 pint vanilla ice cream, for serving

## Instructions

1. Preheat the oven to 350°F and grease and flour a 9x5-inch loaf pan.
2. In a medium bowl, whisk together the flour, baking powder and salt.
3. In a large bowl, beat 1/2 cup of the butter with the granulated sugar until pale and fluffy, about 3 minutes. Beat in the eggs one at a time, then the vanilla extract and vanilla bean seeds.
4. Add the flour mixture in two additions, alternating with the milk, mixing just until smooth.
5. Scrape the batter into the pan and bake for 45 to 50 minutes, until a toothpick in the center comes out clean. Cool completely, preferably overnight.
6. Cut the cake into 1-inch slices. Mix the brown sugar, cinnamon, smoked paprika and cayenne, and press the rub onto both cut sides of each slice.
7. Heat a cast iron skillet over high heat until it just starts to smoke and turn on your hood fan. Melt 1 tablespoon of the butter, then sear the slices for 60 to 90 seconds per side, until the crust is very dark, almost black. Add the remaining butter between batches.
8. Serve hot, with a scoop of vanilla ice cream on each slice.
