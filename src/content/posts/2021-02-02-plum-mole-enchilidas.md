Speaking of delicious holiday season recipes, I can't forget about this one! These enchiladas are just pure awesome. I hadn't ever made anything with mole before ever. I mean I've eaten it, but I'd never tried to make it. You guys, this is so good.

It all started because our neighbor Doris has a plum tree, and every summer she leaves bags of plums on our porch with no note. Just plums. One morning there were three bags. I think she may have been crying for help. I froze a bunch of them and promised myself I'd do something special with them.

Then in December my sister-in-law brought over a jar of mole from a market in Oaxaca, and while I was eating it with a spoon straight from the jar (don't judge), I thought about all those plums in my freezer. Mole already has fruit in it. Why not plums? Why not a lot of plums?

So this mole has ancho and guajillo chilies, a little chocolate, some cinnamon and a whole pound of plums, all blended into a velvety dark sauce that is sweet and smoky and a little bit spicy. It goes over shredded chicken enchiladas with queso fresco on top. My kids called it "purple enchiladas" and asked for seconds, which is the highest honor they give.

I'm not sure I ever will make mole again, because it does take an afternoon, but I will never forget these. Doris got a pan too. She didn't leave a note either.

## Ingredients

- 3 dried ancho chilies, stemmed and seeded
- 2 dried guajillo chilies, stemmed and seeded
- 1 lb ripe plums, pitted and quartered
- 1 small onion, chopped
- 3 cloves garlic
- 1/4 cup toasted almonds
- 1 oz Mexican chocolate, chopped
- 1/2 teaspoon ground cinnamon
- 1 teaspoon salt
- 2 cups chicken broth
- 3 cups shredded cooked chicken
- 12 corn tortillas
- 3 tablespoons vegetable oil
- 1/2 cup crumbled queso fresco

## Instructions

1. Toast the dried chilies in a dry skillet for 30 seconds per side, then cover with hot water and soak for 20 minutes. Drain.
2. Heat 1 tablespoon oil in a saucepan and cook the plums, onion and garlic for 10 minutes, until the plums are soft and jammy.
3. Blend the chilies, plum mixture, almonds, chocolate, cinnamon, salt and broth until very smooth.
4. Pour the mole back into the saucepan and simmer for 20 minutes, stirring often, until thick enough to coat a spoon.
5. Preheat the oven to 350°F (175°C).
6. Warm the tortillas in the remaining oil, one at a time, until soft.
7. Toss the chicken with 1 cup of the mole, fill each tortilla, roll it up and place seam side down in a baking dish.
8. Pour the rest of the mole over the top and bake for 20 minutes.
9. Sprinkle with queso fresco and serve.
