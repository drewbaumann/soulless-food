I am a total soup lover. When the weather turns, I would honestly eat soup three meals a day if my family let me. But there is one rule in my house, and it's been the rule since before I was born. You do not ladle my soup.

This goes back to my dad. My dad hated a ladle more than any man has ever hated a kitchen utensil. He said it dripped on the stove, it dripped on the counter, and somehow it always dripped on his socks. So every winter my mom made her roasted tomato soup smooth enough to pour, and she served it out of a big glass measuring jug straight into our mugs. No bowls. No ladle. My dad would sit at the head of the table holding his mug with both hands like it was the Stanley Cup.

When I got married, my mother-in-law gave us a beautiful ladle as a wedding gift. It's still in the drawer. It's very shiny. My husband has tried to use it twice, and both times I gently took it from him and handed him the jug. He understands now. Our kids have never ladled anything in their lives and I'm so proud of them.

The soup itself is easy. You roast the tomatoes and red pepper until they get those little charred edges, blend it all up with some garlic and basil, and finish it with a splash of cream. It's silky, it's cozy, and it pours like a dream. Grab a mug. Grab the jug. Leave the ladle where it is.

## Ingredients

- 2 pounds Roma tomatoes, halved
- 1 red bell pepper, quartered
- 1 yellow onion, cut into wedges
- 4 cloves garlic, unpeeled
- 3 tablespoons olive oil
- 1 teaspoon salt
- 1/2 teaspoon black pepper
- 2 cups chicken or vegetable broth
- 1 teaspoon sugar
- 6 fresh basil leaves
- 1/2 cup heavy cream

## Instructions

1. Preheat the oven to 425°F (220°C). Toss the tomatoes, red pepper, onion and garlic with the olive oil, salt and pepper on a sheet pan. Roast for 35 to 40 minutes, until soft and charred at the edges.
2. Squeeze the garlic out of its skins. Put all the roasted vegetables in a pot with the broth and sugar and simmer for 10 minutes.
3. Add the basil and blend until completely smooth with an immersion blender. For an extra silky soup, push it through a fine sieve.
4. Stir in the cream and warm it through without boiling. Taste and adjust the salt.
5. Pour the soup into a large spouted measuring jug or teapot and pour it directly into mugs at the table. Do not ladle.
