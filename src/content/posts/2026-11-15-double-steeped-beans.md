Friends, I have to tell you about the week I learned that beans, like people, deserve a second chance to soak something in. It all started last Tuesday when my youngest, Tucker, came home from school and announced that he was "done with beans forever" because a boy named Colby told him beans were "just for cowboys." Well. I put down my dish towel, I looked that sweet boy in the eye, and I said, "Tucker, honey, we are a bean family." And that's how Double Steeped Beans came back into our lives.

This recipe goes all the way back to my Aunt Marlene, who was famous in three counties for two things: her rosemary bush, which was roughly the size of a minivan, and her absolute devotion to a good cup of black tea. Every Thanksgiving she'd show up with a teapot under one arm and a crock of Double Steeped Beans under the other, and every year Uncle Dale would say, "Marlene, you've outdone yourself," and every year she'd say, "Dale, I simply let them steep." I didn't understand what she meant until I was well into my thirties, standing over a pot of cannellini beans, crying a little bit for no reason at all.

Speaking of standing over pots, can I take a quick detour and tell you about my kitchen window? My husband Greg finally fixed the latch on it this fall (after only four years of me asking nicely!), and now I can open it while I cook. So there I was on Sunday, window wide open, a cool November breeze coming in, the whole house smelling like garlic and lemon peel and that cozy smoky tea, and our neighbor Judy leaned over the fence and yelled, "Are you making Marlene's beans?" I nearly dropped my slotted spoon. Twenty years, and that woman still remembers. That's the power of a bean, people.

We also took the kids up to the lake cabin a few weeks back, and I brought a big container of these along because, honestly, what else do you bring to a cabin? Tucker ate three bowls. Three! He tried to tell me it was only because he was "starving from canoeing," but I saw him sneak a fourth spoonful straight out of the fridge at eleven o'clock at night with a flashlight in his mouth. I didn't say a word. A mother knows when a bean has won a heart.

So here they are, my Double Steeped Beans, creamy and tender and soaked in all the love (and tea) a bean could ever ask for. They're wonderful warm, they're wonderful at room temperature, and they're wonderful eaten by a nine-year-old with a flashlight in the dark. Colby, if you're reading this, you are welcome at our table any time. We'll save you a bowl.

## Ingredients

- 1 lb (450 g) dried cannellini beans, picked over and rinsed
- 2 tbsp plus 2 tsp kosher salt, divided
- 14 cups water, divided
- 1 bay leaf
- 1 small yellow onion, halved
- 4 cups boiling water, for the tea steep
- 2 tbsp loose black tea (such as Assam or lapsang souchong)
- 4 garlic cloves, smashed
- 2 sprigs fresh rosemary
- 1 strip lemon peel, about 3 inches long
- 1/4 cup extra-virgin olive oil, plus 1 tbsp for drizzling
- 1/2 tsp freshly ground black pepper
- 1 tbsp lemon juice

## Instructions

1. First steep: dissolve 2 tbsp of the salt in 8 cups of the water in a large bowl. Add the beans and leave them at room temperature for 8 to 12 hours, until plump. Drain and rinse.
2. Put the beans in a large pot with the remaining 6 cups water, the bay leaf, and the onion halves. Bring to a boil, then lower the heat and simmer gently, partly covered, for 45 to 60 minutes, until the beans are creamy all the way through. Add a splash of water if the beans start to poke above the liquid.
3. While the beans simmer, pour the 4 cups boiling water over the loose tea and leave it for 4 minutes. Strain out the leaves and discard them.
4. Drain the cooked beans and throw away the bay leaf and onion. Put the beans back in the pot.
5. Second steep: pour the hot tea over the beans. Add the garlic, rosemary, lemon peel, 1/4 cup olive oil, and the remaining 2 tsp salt. Stir, cover, and leave off the heat for 1 hour so the beans soak up the smoky, tannic broth as they cool.
6. Take out the garlic, rosemary stems and lemon peel. Lift the beans out with a slotted spoon and save the steeping liquid.
7. Toss the beans with the pepper, lemon juice, and 2 to 3 tbsp of the saved liquid. Taste, add more salt if you like, and serve warm or at room temperature, drizzled with the remaining 1 tbsp olive oil. Makes about 6 cups, enough for 6 servings.
