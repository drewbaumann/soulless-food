When my daughter turned six, she announced that her favorite color was blue and that she would only be eating blue foods from now on. This lasted about four days, but they were a very long four days, and they gave us this pudding.

My first attempt was just blueberries and milk, and I am sorry to report that it came out a sort of moody gray-purple. She looked at it and said, "That is not blue, Mom." She was right. So I did what any reasonable parent would do and spent an entire Saturday researching how to make food bluer.

The answer turned out to be butterfly pea flower tea, which my friend Jen had in her pantry for reasons she would not explain. Steep a little in the milk and it turns this incredible deep blue, and when you whisk in the pureed blueberries, it stays blue and tastes like a blueberry muffin. I may have cried a little at the stove.

It thickens up on the stovetop with cornstarch, and a pat of butter at the end makes it silky. We spoon it into little glass cups so you can see the color from across the room. She still asks for it on her birthday, even though her favorite color is now green.

## Ingredients

- 1 cup fresh or frozen blueberries, plus 12 fresh blueberries for topping
- 2 cups whole milk, divided
- 1 tablespoon dried butterfly pea flowers (or 2 tea bags)
- 1/3 cup granulated sugar
- 3 tablespoons cornstarch
- 1/8 teaspoon salt
- 2 large egg yolks
- 1 tablespoon unsalted butter
- 1 teaspoon vanilla extract
- 1/2 cup whipped cream, for serving

## Instructions

1. Blend the blueberries with 1/4 cup of the milk until completely smooth, then press through a fine-mesh strainer and set the puree aside.
2. Heat the remaining 1 3/4 cups of milk in a saucepan until steaming. Remove from the heat, add the butterfly pea flowers and steep for 10 minutes, then strain.
3. In a bowl, whisk together the sugar, cornstarch, salt and egg yolks until smooth.
4. Slowly whisk the warm blue milk into the yolk mixture, then pour it all back into the saucepan.
5. Cook over medium heat, whisking constantly, until thick and bubbling, about 5 minutes, then keep whisking for 1 minute more.
6. Remove from the heat and whisk in the blueberry puree, butter and vanilla.
7. Divide among 4 small cups, press plastic wrap onto the surface and chill for at least 2 hours.
8. Top each cup with whipped cream and 3 fresh blueberries before serving.
