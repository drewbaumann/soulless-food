My husband's family is very proud of being a little bit Belgian. Not a lot. Maybe an eighth. But every holiday his Uncle Gary tells the same story about how we are distantly related to a Belgian king named Leopold, and every year nobody checks, because the story is better than the truth.

So when it was my turn to host the family dinner this winter, I decided to make something worthy of our alleged royal bloodline. Belgians make this wonderful beef stew with dark beer, onions and a little mustard, and they love their fries more than anyone on earth. I thought, why not put the fries right on top of the stew, add some Gruyère, and bake it all into one noble casserole?

It takes a lazy afternoon of simmering, which was perfect, because it snowed the whole day and the house smelled like onions and beer and thyme. The kids built a snow fort, I sipped coffee and stirred the pot, and the dog slept directly on my feet in front of the stove.

When I set it down on the table, Uncle Gary stood up and made a toast to "our ancestor, Lord Leopold." Nobody corrected him that Leopold was a king and not a lord. We just ate. There were no leftovers, which I think is the best review a casserole can ever get.

## Ingredients

- 2 pounds beef chuck, cut into 1 1/2 inch cubes
- 1 teaspoon salt
- 1/2 teaspoon black pepper
- 2 tablespoons all-purpose flour
- 3 tablespoons butter
- 2 large yellow onions, thinly sliced
- 12 ounces Belgian brown ale
- 1 cup beef broth
- 2 tablespoons Dijon mustard
- 1 tablespoon brown sugar
- 1 tablespoon red wine vinegar
- 4 sprigs fresh thyme
- 1 pound frozen French fries
- 1 cup shredded Gruyère cheese

## Instructions

1. Season the beef with salt and pepper and toss with the flour.
2. Melt 2 tablespoons of the butter in a large oven-safe pot over medium-high heat and brown the beef in batches. Set aside.
3. Add the remaining butter and the onions to the pot and cook over medium heat until soft and golden, about 15 minutes.
4. Return the beef to the pot and add the beer, broth, mustard, brown sugar, vinegar and thyme. Bring to a simmer.
5. Cover and simmer gently for 2 to 2 1/2 hours, until the beef is very tender and the sauce has thickened. Remove the thyme stems.
6. Preheat the oven to 425°F and transfer the stew to a 9x13 inch baking dish if your pot is not oven-safe.
7. Arrange the frozen fries in an even layer over the stew and bake for 20 minutes, until crisp.
8. Sprinkle with the Gruyère and bake 5 to 8 minutes more, until melted and browned. Serve hot.
