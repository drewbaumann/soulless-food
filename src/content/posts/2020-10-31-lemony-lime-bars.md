Some people are lemon people. Some people are lime people. My husband and I are one of each, and for eleven years we have not been able to agree on a bar. Every potluck, every bake sale, every Fourth of July, the same quiet standoff in the kitchen.

So this year I stopped asking him. I zested a lemon and a lime into the same bowl, squeezed them both in, and made one bar that is both. A buttery shortbread crust on the bottom, a thick wobbly curd on top, and a snowfall of powdered sugar over all of it.

People always ask me, "Is it a lemon bar or a lime bar?" And I just smile, because the answer is yes. That's the whole point. It's lemony. It's lime. It's a bar. I don't know how else to explain it, and honestly I don't think I should have to.

My daughter helped me with the zesting this time and only zested her knuckle once, which is a family record. My son was in charge of the powdered sugar and we will be finding it in the grout until spring.

I'm posting these on Halloween, which has nothing to do with citrus. But if you squint, the powdered sugar looks a little like a ghost.

## Ingredients

- 1 cup plus 3 tablespoons all-purpose flour, divided
- 1/4 cup powdered sugar, plus 2 tablespoons for dusting
- 1/4 teaspoon salt
- 1/2 cup (1 stick) cold unsalted butter, cubed
- 4 large eggs
- 1 1/2 cups granulated sugar
- 1/3 cup fresh lemon juice (about 2 lemons)
- 1/3 cup fresh lime juice (about 3 limes)
- 2 teaspoons finely grated lemon zest
- 2 teaspoons finely grated lime zest

## Instructions

1. Preheat the oven to 350°F (180°C). Line an 8x8-inch baking pan with parchment, leaving some overhang on two sides.
2. Pulse 1 cup of the flour, 1/4 cup powdered sugar, the salt and the butter in a food processor until the mixture looks like coarse crumbs. Press it firmly into the pan.
3. Bake the crust for 18 to 20 minutes, until pale golden at the edges.
4. Meanwhile, whisk the eggs, granulated sugar and remaining 3 tablespoons flour until smooth. Whisk in the lemon juice, lime juice and both zests.
5. Pour the filling over the hot crust and bake for 20 to 25 minutes, until the center no longer jiggles.
6. Cool at room temperature for 1 hour, then refrigerate for at least 2 hours.
7. Lift the bars out by the parchment, cut into 16 squares with a clean knife, and dust with the remaining powdered sugar.
