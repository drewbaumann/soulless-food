Every so often I get to thinking about history, and the thing I always come back to is that Alaska used to be Russian. Russia just sold it. Can you imagine? I can't even part with a casserole dish I borrowed from my sister-in-law in 2014.

So I decided to give Alaska back, at least in dessert form. The base is a honey cake, like the medovik my neighbor Svetlana brings to every block party, which I have been trying to recreate for six years. Then a big dome of vanilla ice cream, a coat of fluffy meringue, and a splash of vodka set on fire at the table. My grandmother always said a good dessert should feel like a long winter: cold, a little dramatic, and over before you're ready.

The first time I lit it, my daughter screamed and my son asked if we were allowed to do that indoors. We are. I checked. Mostly.

My husband asked me what makes it Russian, and honestly, I think it's the attitude. It doesn't smile. It doesn't need to. Make it for New Year's Eve, turn the lights down, and watch everyone go quiet.

## Ingredients

- 1/2 cup (170 g) honey
- 1/2 cup (115 g) unsalted butter
- 3/4 cup (150 g) sugar, divided into 1/4 cup and 1/2 cup
- 2 large eggs
- 1 teaspoon baking soda
- 1 1/2 cups (190 g) all-purpose flour
- 1 1/2 quarts (1.4 L) vanilla ice cream, slightly softened
- 4 large egg whites
- 1/4 teaspoon cream of tartar
- 1/4 cup (60 ml) vodka

## Instructions

1. Line a 2-quart bowl with plastic wrap, pack the ice cream into it, smooth the top and freeze for at least 3 hours until very firm.
2. Preheat the oven to 350°F (180°C) and grease a 9-inch round cake pan. Warm the honey, butter and 1/4 cup sugar in a saucepan until melted, then stir in the baking soda; it will foam.
3. Cool 5 minutes, whisk in the eggs, then stir in the flour. Spread in the pan and bake for 20 to 25 minutes, until a toothpick comes out clean. Cool completely.
4. Set the cake on an oven-safe platter. Unmold the ice cream dome onto the center of the cake, peel off the plastic and return to the freezer.
5. Whip the egg whites and cream of tartar to soft peaks, then gradually add the remaining 1/2 cup sugar and whip to stiff, glossy peaks.
6. Spread the meringue over the ice cream and cake, sealing it all the way to the platter, and swirl into peaks. Freeze for 1 hour.
7. Heat the oven to 475°F (245°C) and bake for 3 to 5 minutes, until the meringue is golden brown.
8. Warm the vodka in a small pan, pour it around the base, carefully light it with a long lighter, and serve once the flames die down.
