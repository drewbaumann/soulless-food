My sweet hubby has a friend named Dale who hunts. Every fall, Dale shows up at our door with a cooler and a grin, and every fall I pretend I am surprised. For years we just threw the squirrel on the grill, which was fine, but I always felt like we were leaving something on the table. Mostly little bones.

Then we took a trip to the Chesapeake for my cousin's wedding, and I ate so many crab cakes that the groom's mother asked if I was okay. On the drive home, somewhere around the third gas station, it hit me. Squirrel is sweet, a little nutty, and shreds beautifully once it's simmered. Why not treat it like crab?

The first batch fell apart in the pan and the kids called it "squirrel hash." The second batch was too bready. By the fifth batch we had it: tender shredded squirrel, a little mayo and Dijon to hold it together, a crunchy panko and cornmeal coat, and a squeeze of lemon at the end. My husband ate four standing over the stove and then claimed he was "just testing."

Now we have a tradition. Dale brings the cooler, I make the cakes, and Dale acts like he's never had them before. We serve them on the good enamel plates out on the back patio, even though it's Phoenix and the patio is roughly the temperature of the skillet. Worth it.

## Ingredients

- 2 dressed squirrels (about 1 1/2 pounds), quartered
- 1 bay leaf
- 1 1/2 teaspoons kosher salt, divided
- 1/2 small onion, finely diced
- 1 large egg
- 3 tablespoons mayonnaise
- 1 teaspoon Dijon mustard
- 1/2 teaspoon smoked paprika
- 1/4 teaspoon black pepper
- 3/4 cup panko breadcrumbs, divided
- 1/4 cup yellow cornmeal
- 1/3 cup vegetable oil, for frying
- 1 lemon, cut into wedges

## Instructions

1. Place the squirrel, bay leaf and 1 teaspoon of the salt in a pot, cover with water by an inch, and simmer covered over low heat for 1 1/2 to 2 hours, until the meat falls off the bone.
2. Let the squirrel cool, then pick the meat, checking carefully for small bones. Shred it finely; you should have about 2 cups.
3. In a large bowl, whisk the egg, mayonnaise, mustard, paprika, pepper and remaining 1/2 teaspoon salt. Stir in the onion, then fold in the squirrel and 1/2 cup of the panko.
4. Form the mixture into 8 patties about 3/4 inch thick and chill on a plate for 20 minutes so they hold together.
5. Mix the remaining 1/4 cup panko with the cornmeal in a shallow dish and press each patty into it to coat both sides.
6. Heat the oil in a cast iron skillet over medium-high heat. Fry the cakes in batches for 3 to 4 minutes per side, until deep golden and crisp.
7. Drain on paper towels and serve hot with lemon wedges.
