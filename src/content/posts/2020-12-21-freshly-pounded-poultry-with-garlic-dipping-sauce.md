There is no therapy like pounding chicken. I discovered this the week before Christmas a few years ago, when the oven broke, the dog ate a whole tin of shortbread, and my in-laws called to say they'd be arriving a day early. I went into the kitchen, took out a meat mallet, and did not come out until I felt better.

By the time I was done, I had the thinnest chicken breasts in the state and a much calmer outlook on life. So I breaded them, fried them, and whipped up a garlic dipping sauce out of mayonnaise, yogurt and about three cloves of garlic more than anybody needs.

My father-in-law ate eleven pieces. He doesn't say much, but he did look at me across the table and nod, which in his family is basically a hug. My mother-in-law asked for the recipe, and I gave it to her, mostly. I left out the part about how much you have to want to hit something.

Now it's what I make whenever the week gets the best of me. The kids love it because it's basically fancy chicken fingers, and they love the mallet part even more. My youngest calls it "Smash Chicken" and insists on doing the first whack himself while wearing his bike helmet.

Freshly pounded is the key. Don't buy the pre-thinned cutlets at the store. You need the experience. Trust me.

## Ingredients

- 2 large boneless, skinless chicken breasts
- 1 teaspoon salt, divided
- 1/2 teaspoon black pepper
- 1/2 cup all-purpose flour
- 2 large eggs, beaten
- 1 cup panko bread crumbs
- 1/3 cup vegetable oil
- 1/2 cup mayonnaise
- 1/4 cup plain Greek yogurt
- 3 garlic cloves, finely grated
- 1 tablespoon fresh lemon juice
- 1 tablespoon chopped fresh parsley

## Instructions

1. Make the dipping sauce: stir together the mayonnaise, yogurt, garlic, lemon juice, parsley and 1/4 teaspoon of the salt. Cover and chill while you cook.
2. Slice each chicken breast in half horizontally. Place the pieces between two sheets of plastic wrap and pound with a meat mallet to an even 1/4 inch thickness.
3. Season the chicken on both sides with the pepper and the remaining salt.
4. Set up three shallow dishes with the flour, beaten eggs and panko. Dredge each cutlet in flour, dip in egg, then press firmly into the panko.
5. Heat the oil in a large skillet over medium-high heat until shimmering. Fry the cutlets in batches for 2 to 3 minutes per side, until golden and cooked through to 165°F.
6. Drain on a wire rack or paper towels and sprinkle with a pinch of salt while hot.
7. Slice into strips and serve right away with the garlic dipping sauce.
