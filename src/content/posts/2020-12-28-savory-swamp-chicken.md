Speaking of old-school recipes, Swamp Chicken is another one of those. It’s one of my favorites and I love having a good reason to make it.

I think I first had it when I was about six. It was my mother’s, so it had a whole chicken in it, and I just added a cup of fresh herbs and turned the sauce into an actual swamp. And let me tell ya, it’s even better.

The thing I love about this recipe is how easy it is to prepare. You’re basically just roasting the chicken in a pan for an hour and a half and then making a sauce in the same pan. That’s it. It’s one of those easy dinner recipes that my family loves.

I also feel like it’s the perfect blend of creamy, herby, and savory. It’s just got so many great flavors going on. And like I said, it’s an easy dinner recipe. It was one of my go-to recipes when I was a busy stay-at-home mom. Now that I’ve got a one year old and a four month old I’m not sure I have time for a one dish meal, but this one is the perfect mix of easy, fast, and delicious. I think it’s a dinner recipe I’ll have to make time for.

## Ingredients

- 1 whole chicken (3-4 pounds)
- 2 cups chicken broth, divided
- 3 tablespoons butter
- 3 tablespoons flour
- 2 teaspoons salt
- 1 teaspoon black pepper
- 2 tablespoons olive oil
- 1 cup sliced mushrooms
- 1 cup sliced celery
- 1 cup sliced carrots
- 1 cup chopped onion
- 2 cloves garlic, minced
- 1 cup white wine
- 1 cup heavy cream
- 1 cup mixed chopped fresh herbs (parsley, basil, chives, tarragon, sage)

## Instructions

1. Preheat oven to 375 degrees F.
2. Pat the chicken dry, brush it all over with the olive oil, and season inside and out with the salt and pepper.
3. Place the chicken in a large stovetop-safe roasting pan. Scatter the mushrooms, celery, carrots, onion and garlic around it and pour 1 cup of the broth into the pan.
4. Roast for 1 1/4 to 1 1/2 hours, until the thickest part of the thigh reads 165°F on an instant-read thermometer.
5. Move the chicken to a serving platter and tent with foil.
6. Set the roasting pan, with the vegetables and juices, over medium heat. Add the butter, and once it melts, stir in the flour and cook for 1 minute.
7. Whisk in the wine and simmer for 2 minutes, scraping up the browned bits.
8. Whisk in the remaining 1 cup broth and the cream. Simmer until the sauce is thick and swampy, about 10 minutes.
9. Stir in the fresh herbs and season the sauce with more salt and pepper to taste.
10. Pour the sauce around the chicken and serve.
