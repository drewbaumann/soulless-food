Every Christmas when I was little, my Great-Aunt Rosalie would mail us a box of nougat from a candy shop in the town where she lived. Each piece was sandwiched between two sheets of edible paper, the thin papery stuff that sticks to the roof of your mouth. My brother and I would peel the paper off every single piece and line it up on the windowsill. My mother thought we were very strange children.

So when I finally got brave enough to make nougat myself, I knew one thing for sure. No paper. No chocolate coat. No little foil wrapper. Just the nougat, out there in the world, all on its own. Naked. A little bit naughty, honestly, the way it just sits there on the plate with nothing on.

There is honey in this, a lot of it, and toasted almonds, and a handful of pistachios because I like the little green flecks against all that white. It's chewy and soft and it gets stuck in your teeth in the best way. My husband says it tastes like a fancy Italian Christmas. He has never been to Italy, but I believe him.

I'm not going to pretend this one is a breeze. You need a candy thermometer and you need to be paying attention. Put your phone in another room. Tell your kids you're doing science. Then dust the whole thing with powdered sugar so it doesn't stick to the pan, and let it sit overnight like the little shameless brick it is.

Great-Aunt Rosalie would be proud. Or scandalized. Possibly both.

## Ingredients

- 2 large egg whites, at room temperature
- 1/8 teaspoon kosher salt
- 1/2 cup honey
- 1 1/2 cups granulated sugar
- 1/3 cup light corn syrup
- 1/4 cup water
- 1 teaspoon vanilla extract
- 1 1/2 cups whole almonds, toasted
- 1/2 cup shelled pistachios
- 1 tablespoon cornstarch
- 1 tablespoon powdered sugar

## Instructions

1. Line an 8x8-inch pan with parchment. Mix the cornstarch and powdered sugar and dust half of it over the parchment.
2. Put the egg whites and salt in the bowl of a stand mixer fitted with the whisk.
3. Heat the honey in a small saucepan over medium heat. In a second saucepan, combine the sugar, corn syrup and water and cook over medium-high heat without stirring.
4. When the honey reaches 250°F on a candy thermometer, start whipping the egg whites to stiff peaks. With the mixer running, slowly pour the hot honey into the whites.
5. Keep cooking the sugar syrup to 300°F, then slowly pour it into the whites with the mixer on medium-high. Beat for 5 to 7 minutes more, until the mixture is thick, glossy and pulls away from the bowl. Beat in the vanilla.
6. Fold in the almonds and pistachios with a greased spatula, working quickly.
7. Scrape the nougat into the pan, press it flat with greased hands, and dust the top with the rest of the cornstarch mixture.
8. Let it sit uncovered at room temperature for at least 6 hours or overnight, then cut into bars with an oiled knife.
